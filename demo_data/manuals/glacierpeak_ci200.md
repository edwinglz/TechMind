# Glacier Peak Dispense CI-200 Countertop Ice/Beverage Dispenser: Service Manual

Model CI-200. Countertop ice and beverage dispenser with integrated carbonator, 8 beverage valves, a 250 lb ice bin with agitator, and a cold plate cast into the bin floor. Rev. 3.2.

## System overview

The CI-200 combines ice storage, ice dispensing, and post-mix beverage dispensing in one cabinet. Water is carbonated on board, cooled through the cold plate, and mixed with syrup at each valve.

### Water supply

Incoming potable water enters through a 3/8 in. inlet at the rear, passes the inlet strainer screen, and is split: one branch feeds the carbonator pump, the other feeds the plain-water valve. A water filter cartridge upstream of the unit is strongly recommended (customer-supplied or Glacier Peak kit P-WFILT).

### CO2 and regulators

CO2 comes from a cylinder outside the cabinet. The **primary regulator** on the cylinder feeds the carbonator. A **secondary regulator** (P-REG2) feeds the bag-in-box syrup pumps at a lower pressure. The cylinder gauge shows remaining supply; when the needle is in the red zone the cylinder is nearly empty.

### Carbonator pump

A rotary vane carbonator pump driven by a 1/3 hp motor pushes water into the stainless carbonator tank, where it absorbs CO2 under pressure. A level probe in the tank switches the pump on and off. A check valve (P-CHKV) at the pump inlet stops carbonated water from flowing backward into the supply line. If the pump cannot fill the tank, it runs continuously and drinks pour flat.

### Bag-in-box syrup pumps

Each flavor has a gas-driven bag-in-box (BIB) pump on the syrup rack. The pump draws syrup from the bag through a quick-disconnect connector (P-BIBC) and sends it to the cold plate. When a bag empties, the pump stalls and that flavor pours as plain carbonated water.

### Cold plate and ice bin

Carbonated water and syrup run through stainless coils cast into an aluminium cold plate in the floor of the ice bin. Ice resting on the plate chills the drinks. If ice does not touch the plate (an ice bridge forms, or meltwater pools because the drain is blocked), drinks pour warm. The ice agitator (P-AGIT) turns the ice to keep it loose and feeds the ice chute.

### Valves and nozzles

Eight post-mix valves mix syrup and carbonated water at the set ratio (brix). Each valve has a removable nozzle and diffuser (P-NOZ) and is sealed with a valve seal and O-ring kit (P-VSEAL). A 24 V solenoid (P-SOL) opens the syrup and water paths when the lever is pressed.

## Installation and pressures

| Item | Setting |
|---|---|
| Incoming water pressure | 40-50 psi dynamic, minimum 25 psi |
| CO2 primary regulator (carbonator) | 90-100 psi |
| CO2 secondary regulator (BIB pumps) | 60-70 psi |
| Carbonator pump run time per fill | under 60 seconds |
| Target drink temperature | 40 degrees F or colder |
| Power | 115 V, 60 Hz, dedicated 15 A circuit |

- Install the CO2 cylinder upright and chained to a wall in a ventilated area.
- Leave 6 in. of clearance at the back for the water inlet and drain.
- The bin drain must run downhill to an open drain with an air gap.

## Brix calibration

Brix is the syrup-to-water ratio. The standard ratio is 5 parts water to 1 part syrup (5:1) unless the syrup supplier says otherwise.

1. Remove the nozzle and diffuser from the valve.
2. Fit the syrup separator tool so syrup and water flow into separate chambers of the brix cup.
3. Pour for 4 seconds. Read the levels on the cup.
4. Turn the syrup flow control (left screw on the valve body) until the ratio matches. Clockwise reduces syrup.
5. Remove the separator, refit the nozzle, and taste-test.

Recheck brix after replacing a valve, a BIB pump, or the secondary regulator.

## Daily and weekly cleaning

**Daily (store staff):**
- Pull off each nozzle and diffuser by hand, soak in warm water with sanitizer for 10 minutes, rinse, and refit.
- Wipe the valve undersides and the splash area.
- Empty and rinse the drip tray.

**Weekly (store staff):**
- Check that the bin drain flows freely by pouring a pitcher of warm water into the empty bin corner.
- Check the ice level and that the agitator turns freely.

**Quarterly (technician):**
- Empty, wash, and sanitize the ice bin.
- Check pressures against the installation table.
- Inspect the carbonator pump inlet strainer and replace the water filter.

## Troubleshooting

### No carbonation (flat drinks)

| Symptom | Likely cause | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Flat on all valves, cylinder gauge in red | CO2 cylinder empty | Swap to a full cylinder |
| Flat on all valves, gauge normal | Cylinder valve closed after a swap | Open the cylinder valve fully |
| Flat on all valves, pump runs constantly or hums | Carbonator pump not filling the tank | Check inlet water pressure; replace pump motor assembly (P-CPUMP) |
| Flat on all valves, pump cycles on and off rapidly | Low water inlet pressure or clogged inlet strainer | Check supply pressure; clean strainer (P-STRN) |
| Flat and warm | Drinks warm, so CO2 breaks out | See Warm drinks |

### Watery or syrupy drinks

| Symptom | Likely cause | Action |
|---|---|---|
| One flavor clear or tastes like soda water | Bag-in-box empty or disconnected | Replace the bag and reconnect firmly |
| One flavor watery or too sweet, bag OK | Brix drifted | Recalibrate brix |
| All flavors watery | Secondary regulator set low; BIB pumps weak | Check secondary pressure (60-70 psi) |
| All flavors watery and flat | Carbonator not filling | See No carbonation |

### No flow on one valve

| Likely cause | Action |
|---|---|
| Nozzle clogged | Soak and clean nozzle and diffuser |
| Solenoid coil failed | Replace solenoid (P-SOL) |
| Syrup line kinked | Straighten line behind the splash panel |

### No flow on all valves

| Likely cause | Action |
|---|---|
| Unit unplugged or breaker tripped | Check power; reset breaker once |
| Water supply shut off | Open the supply valve |
| Cold plate frozen (after an ice-bin overfill with no drain) | Let thaw, then clear drain |

### Foaming

| Likely cause | Action |
|---|---|
| Drinks too warm | See Warm drinks |
| Primary CO2 pressure too high | Set primary regulator to 90-100 psi |
| Dirty nozzle | Clean nozzle and diffuser |

### Warm drinks

| Symptom | Likely cause | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Ice bin low | Not enough ice on the cold plate | Fill the bin |
| Ice in one solid block, not touching the plate | Ice bridge | Break up the ice so it rests on the plate |
| Water pooling in the bin | Drain clogged or drain tube cracked | Clear drain; replace drain tube (P-DRAIN) |

Ice bridging and drain problems are most common in hot weather and high-volume periods.

### Leaks

| Symptom | Likely cause | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Nozzle drips after pouring; valve keeps running | Worn valve seal or O-ring | Replace seal and O-ring kit (P-VSEAL) |
| Water under the unit | Bin drain tube cracked or disconnected | Replace drain tube |
| Hissing or spraying at the carbonator | Relief valve weeping or over-pressure | Check primary pressure; replace relief valve (P-RELV) |

### No ice

| Symptom | Likely cause | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Bin empty | Ice not refilled | Fill the bin |
| Ice clumped into a block | Ice fused in hot weather | Empty and break up the ice |
| Agitator does not turn, grinding noise | Agitator motor failed or chute gate jammed | Free the gate; replace agitator motor (P-AGIT) or chute assembly (P-ICECH) |

## Safety

- **CO2 is an asphyxiant.** CO2 is heavier than air and collects in low, enclosed spaces such as back rooms and basements. If a leak is suspected (hissing, headache, dizziness, shortness of breath), ventilate and leave the area, and do not re-enter until it has been aired out.
- **High-pressure gas.** Never loosen fittings on a pressurized CO2 line, regulator, or carbonator tank. Close the cylinder valve and bleed pressure first.
- **Electrical.** Unplug the unit before opening any panel or touching wet areas near the electrical box. Only qualified technicians may open panels.
- **Sanitation.** Mold in nozzles and a dirty ice bin are a food-safety hazard. Sanitize before serving.
